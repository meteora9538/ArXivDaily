#### Graph Neural Networks for Astrophysics and Cosmology
 - **Authors:** Christian Kragh Jespersen
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Machine Learning (cs.LG)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27051

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27051

 - **Abstract**
 Physics seldom makes breakthroughs when we consider objects only in isolation. Instead we model and measure how objects sit among their neighbours, spatial, temporal, or otherwise: a galaxy in its environment, a halo in its history, a photon hit in a detector. Graphs are ideally suited for encoding exactly these relations, and Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) learn the geometric functions that respect them. This review presents the use of GNNs for astrophysics and cosmology, and argues that GNNs often suit physics and physical data better than any alternative: they fit the native geometry of the domain and the signal, enable the incorporation of the symmetries and prior knowledge we already trust and have toiled to build, and stay flexible enough to capture what we do not yet understand. GNNs' physically intuitive workings also render them more interpretable than other deep-learning architectures. We show how we can begin from general principles of the geometry of deep learning and build the graph and the graph neural network from the ground up, and how other architectures, like the convolutional network and the transformer, can be seen as special cases of one unified geometric blueprint. We survey applications across cosmology, galaxy formation, dynamics, and instrumentation; and we chart the frontier. From this survey, we find that only one in six articles outside instrumentation applies GNNs to data rather than just simulations. We also distil a set of best practices, including when not to reach for a graph. Throughout, we will prioritise physical intuition and foundational principles over mathematical machinery whenever possible.

 - **Abstract**
 Luminosity functions (LF) are some of the most basic quantities providing insights into galaxy evolution across cosmic time and enabling a direct comparison between observations and simulations. Here, we present the first [CII]158$\mu$m LF at $z\sim7$ from a sizeable UV-selected ALMA sample. Our analysis makes use of 27 galaxies with significant [CII] emission line detections in the epoch of reionization from the REBELS (Reionization Era Bright Emission Line Survey) ALMA Large Program and its pilot programs. We used these line detections and similar from ALPINE to calibrate UV luminosity-$L_{\mathrm{[CII]}}$ and stellar mass-$L_{\mathrm{[CII]}}$ relations at $z\sim7$. Given the challenge in precisely defining a selection volume for the REBELS sample itself, we rely on published UV LF and stellar mass function (SMF) determinations at $z\sim7$ and three complementary approaches to derive the [CII] LF. The UV LF-based method provides a lower limit on the [CII] LF, due to its missing the most dust-obscured systems. Our SMF-based estimate yields a Schechter function with a characteristic luminosity of $L^*_{\mathrm{[CII]}}=10^{9.11^{+0.11}_{-0.27}} L_\odot$ and a steep faint-end slope. From $z\sim7$ to $z\sim5$, our inferred [CII] LF shows an evolution of +0.3 dex in normalisation and +0.1 dex in $L^*_{\mathrm{[CII]}}$. Our results indicate a general underprediction from models of the abundance of [CII] emitters at high-redshift and thus potentially the number densities of massive yet UV-faint systems implied by our SMF-based method. Additionally, they suggest that 63% of the star formation rate density occurs in massive [CII]-bright systems and that $\sim$60-90% of [CII] emitters at $z\sim7$ may be missed by bright UV selections. This highlights the importance of surveys focusing on especially massive, UV-faint sources at $z>6$.

 - **Abstract**
 Understanding how stars form at low metallicity is challenging because carbon monoxide (CO) emission, which usually probes the molecular gas reservoir, is faint, and tends to be concentrated in parsec-scale clumps. To overcome this difficulty, in order to study the molecular clouds in metal-poor environments, we have acquired CO(2-1) ALMA maps at 3-4 pc resolution of four nearby dwarf galaxies with nebular (O/H) metallicities ranging from roughly 1/9 Z_sun to 1/3 Z_sun. Significant CO signal in the cubes has been identified through two masks, a ``strict'', high-confidence, mask and a ``broad'' one for better completeness. The former traces the cores of the clouds, while the latter includes fainter emission in the vicinity of the cores. Through a cloud-finding algorithm applied to the two sets of masked cubes, we have identified more than 200 molecular clouds across the sample, with roughly 50 in NGC3109, at 1/9 Z_sun, the lowest metallicity parsec-scale cloud population yet observed. The best-fit size-linewidth relations for the resolved clouds are similar among all galaxies, with a slope like the Milky Way (MW), but with lower velocity dispersion at a given cloud size. For a fixed CO-to-H_2 MW conversion factor, the cloud virial ratios tend to be large, and show a weak increasing trend with increasing cloud size, and decreasing apparent surface densities and metallicity. We find evidence for faint but significant CO emission outside CO-bright cores, spanning similar velocities to those observed in the cores. We attribute this to effective shielding by gas and dust, despite the low metallicities of our sample, since the CO tends to be cospatial with dust as traced by far-infrared emission.
#### Beyond static torques: Self-consistent migration and dynamical trapping of low-mass planets in dusty disks
 - **Authors:** Jose L. Gomez, Pablo Benitez-Llambay, Ximena S. Ramos, Octavio M. Guilera
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27071

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27071

 - **Abstract**
 The rapid inward migration of low-mass planets remains a primary theoretical challenge in planet formation. While recent studies show that the solid component of protoplanetary disks can exert strong positive torques to counteract the negative gas torques, traditional static fixed-orbit models fail to capture the fully coupled dynamics of a migrating planet. In this work, we present 2D high-resolution, two-fluid hydrodynamical simulations using a comoving reference frame to track the self-consistent orbital evolution of embedded planets across a comprehensive six-dimensional parameter space. By allowing the planet to dynamically respond to disk-exerted torques, we identify a wide diversity of migration regimes, involving inward/outward migration and oscillatory radial confinement. We also find that massive cores can overcome dust-induced barriers, experiencing dust-mediated runaway outward or inward migration. Conversely, in dust-rich environments continuous gas-dust momentum exchange combined with planet torques dynamically regenerates co-orbital dust asymmetries, creating robust dynamical attractors where super-Earths undergo stable, oscillatory confinement, effectively halting inward drift without requiring pre-existing pressure bumps or disk substructures. This self-induced trapping mechanism could be relevant in the outer disk, beyond the water-ice line in environments with a enhanced local dust-to-gas ratio, where icy pebbles are moderately coupled to the gas. In low-metallicity or highly diffusive disks, planets generally adopt monotonic inward decay, apart from cases with outward migration. Ultimately, the diversity of these self-consistent trajectories demonstrates that static torque approximations are insufficient. Evaluating fully coupled kinematic feedback is essential for accurately predicting planetary survival and the final architecture of planetary systems.

 - **Abstract**
 We present Leviathan, a sub-millimetre galaxy (SMG) spectroscopically confirmed at $z=4.051$ with JWST/NIRSpec. Modelling 31 bands of photometry from the rest-frame ultraviolet to the millimetre, we find a stellar mass of $\log(M_\star/M_\odot)=11.34^{+0.15}_{-0.22}$, making Leviathan one of the most massive spectroscopically confirmed galaxies at $z>4$, yet consistent with $\Lambda$CDM for a star-formation efficiency of approximately 20%. Although SMGs are often thought to be building the dense cores of compact quiescent galaxies, Leviathan is extended, with a rest-frame $0.9\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ half-light radius of 4.3 kpc. Leviathan forms stars on the main sequence (approximately $400\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$) with dust spread across its disc. Both its stellar mass and dust are more extended than those of $z\approx3$ SMGs. Alongside GN20.2b, which has a similar mass and star-formation rate but is three times smaller, these show that massive dusty galaxies at $z\approx4$ can have very different stellar structures, and that not all have their stellar mass concentrated in a compact core.

 - **Abstract**
 Understanding gas accretion and feedback in low-mass galaxy groups bridges the gap between isolated ellipticals and cluster-dominant galaxies. We present new Multi Unit Spectroscopic Explorer (MUSE) observations of 17 nearby brightest group galaxies (BGGs), revealing diverse origins for their gas reservoirs. Warm ionized gas, traced by Halpha and [N II], is detected in 77% (13/17) of the sample, with morphologies including rotating disks with or without tails and diffuse emission, rings, and filaments. Unlike brightest cluster galaxies, most BGGs host regularly rotating stars, whereas their gas displays diverse kinematics. Nearly half of the rotating gas disks are aligned with the stars, consistent with internal recycling. However, tails, diffuse emission, or rings in three aligned systems may retain signatures of past accretion or interactions. No single mechanism dominates: BGGs are transitional systems in which internal recycling, environmental accretion, and cooling-driven condensation coexist, linking isolated ellipticals to cluster-dominant galaxies.

 Extremely Metal Poor (XMP) galaxies are the least chemically evolved objects in the local Universe and provide important constraints on galaxy formation in low-metallicity environments. This work investigates the ionised gas kinematics and star formation properties of XMP galaxies to identify disturbed or multi-component gas motions and explore their connection with star formation.  - **Authors:** Marcus L. Marcussen, Mikkel N. Lund

 - **Abstract**
 Asteroseismology relies on disk-integrated photometry or radial velocities, in which geometric cancellation suppresses modes of high spherical degree ($l\geq4$). #### Dynamical stability of Magnetic Supramassive Neutron Stars
 - **Authors:** Jorge L. Rodríguez-Monteverde, Sebastiano Bernuzzi, William Cook, Maximilian Jacobi, Brynmor Haskell

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28609

 - **Abstract**
 We investigate the dynamical stability of strongly magnetized supramassive neutron stars close to the mass-shedding limit using fully general-relativistic magnetohydrodynamic simulations in three spatial dimensions. Equilibrium configurations are constructed using a microphysical equation of state and considering poloidal magnetic fields up to maximum strengths ${\sim} 10^{17}\,\mathrm{G}$. The initially unstable magnetic fields reorganize into mixed poloidal--toroidal configurations on Alfvén timescales shorter than the stellar rotation period.
