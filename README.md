 - **Abstract**
 We present SIGNN++, a learning-based star identification (SID) framework for celestial navigation in GNSS-denied nighttime environments. Although researchers have developed learning-based SID methods to improve robustness to observation noise, prior evaluations have focused mainly on synthetic or non-terrestrial environments, leaving their reliability under real terrestrial conditions unclear. To address these limitations, SIGNN++ extends SIGNN, a graph neural network-based SID method, with three components: a more realistic noise model for training data, Fourier Transform (FT) of angular-distance edge features, and RANSAC-based Perspective Star Alignment for identification and attitude refinement.  - **Abstract**
 Whether the formation of the Galactic bar was connected to the Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus (GSE) merger, the last significant merger experienced by the Milky Way, remains an open question. The bar formation epoch has been inferred from the star formation history of the nuclear stellar disc (NSD) traced by Mira variables, and the GSE merger epoch from the age-metallicity relation of red giants. However, these epochs rest on different stellar tracers with different age scales and cannot be compared directly. We recalibrate the Mira period-age relation onto the age scale of APOGEE red giants, with ages from a Bayesian neural network trained on Kepler asteroseismic ages. If NSD star formation traces bar formation, these coincidences suggest a possible scenario in which the GSE seeded the bar formation, while fresh gas supplied by the GSE and outer disc gas driven inward by its tidal torques, increased the baryonic dominance of the inner Galaxy.

 Titan's upper atmospheric layers provide a unique opportunity to study how photochemistry, photoionisation, haze microphysics and escape processes shape its atmospheric chemical structure, providing a unique benchmark for understanding these processes in exoplanets. Using a 1D first-principles approach, we extend the Kompot model to simulate Titan's upper atmosphere and investigate its thermal and chemical structure, and Jeans escape.  - **Authors:** Parth Mehrotra, Aaron Smith, Yuan Bian, William McClymont, Ewald Puchwein, Rahul Kannan, Laura Keating, Xuejian Shen, Mark Vogelsberger, Sandro Tacchella, Matthew Hayes, Crystal L. Martin, Cody Carr, Anand Utsav Kapoor, Shyam Menon, Alaina Henry
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30265

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30265

 - **Abstract**
 The ionizing radiation that galaxies release into their surroundings depends on whether efficient Lyman continuum (LyC) escape coincides with strong photon production. Stellar feedback can open escape paths as the source population fades, linking the escaping radiation to a galaxy's recent history. We investigate this timing in thirteen high-redshift (z > 3) target haloes from the THESAN-ZOOM radiation-hydrodynamic simulations, post-processed with COLT radiative transfer and sampled at ~10 Myr intervals. The clearest delayed relationship links $\text{SFR}_{10}/\text{SFR}_{100}$ to the LyC escape fraction. Selecting the nearest positive cross-correlation peak gives typical lags of ~16 Myr and peak correlations of 0.4-0.6 in massive haloes, with longer and less coherent delays towards lower masses. The fitted delays scale approximately as $M_\star^{-0.19}$ and $M_\text{halo}^{-0.27}$, although the shortest lags approach the native snapshot spacing. Recurrent variations span multiple amplitude-dependent timescales, consistent with repeated episodes of star formation and feedback. Recent star formation closely tracks both intrinsic and escaping photon rates, while the escape fraction reflects the evolving gas transparency. The H$\alpha$-to-UV luminosity ratio retains a delayed association with escape, offering an observable connection between burst phase and leakage. High transparency during a fading phase can nevertheless yield little escaping radiation. The temporal overlap of photon production and transmission therefore shapes both the amount and timing of escaping radiation, informing the interpretation of observed leakers and their contribution to cosmic reionization.
#### Density-split analyses of the pairwise kinetic Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect
 - **Authors:** Finn A. Roper, Aritra K. Gon, Yan-Chuan Cai, John A. Peacock
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30267

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30267

 - **Abstract**
 We consider the dependence of the kinetic Sunyaev-Zeldovich (kSZ) effect on the environments of foreground cosmological structures. Standard pairwise kSZ estimators exploit the fact that galaxies below separations of $\sim 200\,h^{-1}{\rm M}_\odot$ fall towards each other on average, yielding a negative mean pairwise kSZ signal. However, this signal is the residual from a near-cancellation between outflows from underdense regions and infall towards overdensities giving a large variance in the pairwise velocity distribution and hence a low signal-to-noise ratio for the mean kSZ signal. This effect is especially marked at the largest scales, where the pairwise velocity distribution becomes more symmetric about zero. We show that splitting galaxies according to their local overdensity allows the convergence and divergence flows to be separated, yielding pairwise kSZ signals of opposing sign, with a reduction in variance for each subset. We use simulations to perform a detailed analysis of this phenomenon, comparing the cosmological information content in the global pairwise kSZ signal with our new density-split pairwise kSZ estimator. We consider galaxy catalogues comparable to datasets from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument, together with cosmic microwave background instrumental noise characteristic of recent and planned ground-based experiments. We predict that the density-split pairwise kSZ will increase the significance of detection by only a modest factor; nevertheless, we expect improvements by factors of respectively 11 and 4 in constraining $\sigma_8$ and $\Omega_{\rm m}$ when using the combination of Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument and Simons Observatory Large Aperture Telescope surveys.

The properties of dust grains are expected to change significantly in dense molecular clouds due to shielding from interstellar radiation and increased frequency of grain-grain and grain-gas collisions. We measure the dust extinction curve shape parameter R(V) in 9 nearby, dense molecular clouds using data from the APOGEE spectroscopic survey, in combination with ten-band photometry from PS1, 2MASS, and WISE.  - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30515

 - **Abstract**
 We examine the long-term evolution of localized eccentricity perturbations in a softened Laplace-Lagrange chain. The secular coupling between mass elements is long-ranged, suggesting that any disturbance imposed at a single radius should disperse rapidly across the chain. We show that this expectation fails once the secular interaction is regularized by a finite softening length $\beta$: while broad eccentricity profiles propagate smoothly, as textbook secular theory anticipates, narrow profiles remain confined to their launching site for millions of secular periods. The confined perturbation is flanked by an apsidally polarized (oppositely-aligned) response, which we show to be the real-space imprint of this spectral projection.  - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31482

 - **Abstract**
