#### A Composite Rest-Frame UV Spectrum of Galaxies at $5.7 < z < 6.9$: Ionization, Outflows, and Implications for Intergalactic Metal Enrichment
 - **Authors:** David DePalma, Robert A. Simcoe, Anna-Christina Eilers, Rongmon Bordoloi, Daichi Kashino, Gauri Kotiwale, Jorryt Matthee
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33154

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33154

 - **Abstract**
 We present a stacked JWST/NIRSpec spectrum of 104 galaxies at $\lambda_{\rm rest}=1200\unicode{x2013}2000\;\mathrm{\mathring{A}}$ to study nebular emission and absorption from the interstellar medium (ISM) and stellar winds. The sample is drawn from known [O III] emitters with mean redshift $z=6.50$, absolute UV magnitude $M_\mathrm{UV}=-20.7$, and stellar mass $M_{\star}=10^{8.93}\;\mathrm{M_\odot}$. The stacked continuum closely resembles lower-redshift Lyman Break Galaxy composites, with a few notable differences. Ly$\;\alpha$ has comparable strength and is redshifted relative to the [O III] systemic frame, consistent with production in gaseous outflows. High-ionization emission lines including O III], C III], and He II are slightly stronger than in lower-redshift stacks but exhibit narrow widths, disfavoring ionization from a broad-line AGN. Emission-line ratios slightly favor ionization by a hard stellar continuum, though a contribution from a narrow-line nuclear source cannot be strictly excluded. Numerous strong absorption lines at both high and low ionization are blueshifted by $\sim200\;\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, again indicating outflows driven by stellar winds or entrained metal-enriched ISM. A simple model suggests that at $z\sim6.5$ most star-forming galaxies are too young for winds at these velocities to propagate beyond $\sim3\unicode{x2013}9$ proper kpc, implying that the observed [O III] emitter population alone cannot explain the frequency of heavy-element absorbers seen in quasar spectra. In this picture, early enrichment of the intergalactic medium likely arises from undetected low-mass galaxies ($M_\star\sim10^5\unicode{x2013}10^6\;\mathrm{M_\odot}$) clustered around the more massive systems detected in JWST/NIRCam WFSS surveys.

 - **Abstract**
 Heavily dust-obscured active galactic nuclei (AGN) are crucial for understanding supermassive black hole growth. Using multi-band MIRI photometry from the MINERVA survey, we report the discovery in the UDS field of an extremely mid-infrared bright Compton-thick AGN (CT AGN) at $z \sim 3.8$, \textit{el Ascua} (the Ember). The AGN and/or its host galaxy is detected at 870\,$\mu$m with ALMA and at 1.4\,GHz with the VLA. With UV-to-radio spectral energy distribution modeling, we estimate that the dusty host galaxy has a stellar mass of $\log\left( M_\star / M_\odot \right)=10.82 \pm 0.57$ and a star formation rate of ${\rm{SFR}} =157 \pm 8 \,M_\odot\,{\rm yr^{-1}}$, and we confirm the presence of a powerful dusty AGN with an intrinsic accretion luminosity of $\log\left( L_{\rm{acc}} /{\rm erg\,s^{-1}} \right)=47.12 \pm 0.58$. With an extremely high ratio between black hole accretion rate and SFR, the AGN is likely nearing the peak of a deeply obscured phase of black hole growth before AGN feedback blows away the obscuring material. Our work demonstrates the power of MIRI to reveal heavily obscured AGN missing from our supermassive black hole census.
#### How Steep is your Dust? Direct Measurements of Nebular Attenuation Curves at Cosmic Noon with JWST
 - **Authors:** Gabriel Maheson, Sirio Belli, Sandro Tacchella, Letizia Bugiani, Joel Leja, Rebecca Davies, Minjung Park, Amir H. Khoram, Matteo Sapori, Caterina Liboni, Benjamin P. Collins
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33166

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33166

 - **Abstract**
 Tracing the shape of the nebular attenuation curve, which is crucial for accurately measuring intrinsic emission-line fluxes, has proven challenging both in the local universe and at high redshift. Here we present nebular dust attenuation curves measured directly for 24 star-forming galaxies ($9.3<\log(\rm M{\star}/M_\odot)<11.1$) at cosmic noon ($1.8<z<3.3$) from the Blue Jay survey. Using deep JWST/NIRSpec spectra, we measure up to eight hydrogen recombination lines from H$\delta$ to Pa$\beta$ per galaxy and find little diversity in the curve shape, with most galaxies agreeing with the \citet{calzetti_dust_2000} attenuation curve. For over $90\%$ of the sample, the nebular reddening from the Balmer lines is consistent with that from the Paschen lines. Using \texttt{Prospector} spectral energy distribution (SED) fits, we obtain the stellar reddening and find that the difference between stellar and nebular reddening increases with stellar mass, and that each reddening measurement is independent of inclination, which disagrees with the two-component dust model. We then measure the impact of using the derived nebular attenuation curve or a literature curve when determining metallicity using the strong-line method, and find that for some diagnostics (e.g., S32, S3, S23, and S3O3), the difference can be as large as $5\sigma$. Caution is needed when using these diagnostics if the nebular attenuation curve cannot be directly measured, since the resulting uncertainty can exceed the measurement uncertainty on the emission-line fluxes even with high signal-to-noise Balmer decrement measurements. These results show that the shape of the nebular attenuation curve is poorly understood and that constraining it is important for reducing systematic uncertainty in galaxy measurements.

 - **Authors:** Letizia Bugiani, Andrew B. Newman, Sirio Belli, Cosimo Marconcini, Pietro Bergamini, Sai Gangula, Claudio Grillo, Andrea Bolamperti, Katherine E. Whitaker, Aliza Beverage, Richard S. Ellis, Amir H. Khoram, Allison Matthews, Marco Lombardi

 - **Abstract**
 We analyze JWST/NIRSpec IFU spectroscopy of MRG-M0150, a gravitationally lensed, massive quiescent galaxy at $z=2.6$ from the INQUEST-JWST survey. Thanks to the combination of lensing magnification with JWST's angular resolution, we are able to build spatially resolved maps of the ionized gas spatial distribution, kinematics, and ionization properties in this system. We detect ionized gas emission throughout the galaxy: the lack of residual star formation inferred from line-ratio diagnostic diagrams, together with the decoupling between the gas and stellar kinematics, suggest that the ionized gas is in a wind. We use the 3D gas kinematics modeling code \texttt{MOKA3D} to constrain the geometry and physical properties of the ionized gas wind. We find that the observations are consistent with a biconical wind driven by the low-luminosity AGN at the center of the galaxy, which is detected in X-rays. The wind reaches high velocities of $1000-1400$ km/s, implying that part of the gas may escape the halo. The ionized gas mass outflow rate, $(0.6 \pm 0.2)$~${\rm M}_{\odot}$~yr${}^{-1}$, is modest but exceeds the upper limit on the residual star formation rate, and it is likely that the wind is multiphase and carries more mass. We show that typical unresolved observations would not detect the wind, suggesting that the incidence of such outflows may be higher than previously recognized. Simple geometric arguments show that the observed outflow must have been launched $\approx 0.45$~Myr ago from the time of observation. This evidence suggests that low-level AGN activity is an important mechanism for maintaining quiescence in massive galaxies at cosmic noon, potentially over cosmological timescales.
#### Fast Outflow Driven from the Magnetically Arrested Disc
 - **Authors:** Taili Yi, Jia-Wen Li
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33443

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33443

 - **Abstract**
 Fast outflows are commonly observed in accreting black hole systems, yet their launching mechanism remains debated, especially when the gas is highly ionized and radiation line driving becomes inefficient. We investigate magnetically driven outflows from magnetically arrested discs (MADs) using a steady, axisymmetric magnetocentrifugal outflow model. The outflow equations follow the classical trans-magnetosonic MHD framework, while the disc structural scalings and outflow-base conditions are motivated by general relativistic magnetohydrodynamics (GRMHD) simulations. We solve for the trans-magnetosonic outflow solutions along prescribed large-scale poloidal magnetic field lines. The outflows launched from the inner disc are strongly accelerated and the formal Newtonian solutions can enter the mildly relativistic regime. The outflow velocity decreases systematically with increasing footpoint radius, producing a radially stratified outflow in which inner streamlines reach UFO-like velocities while outer streamlines form slower components. We also find that the MAD solutions have a large magnetic lever arm $\tilde{l}=(R_{\rm A}/R)^2$ is typically several tens and can reach $\sim10^2$, which implies that the outflow can carry a large amount of the specific angular momentum of the disc material at its footpoint, and we find that about $55\%$ of the angular-momentum estimated between the outer and inner disc boundaries is removed by the magnetic outflow. The total mass-loss rate in the outflow is about $14\%$ of the disc mass-accretion rate in our fiducial model. These results suggest that the MADs provide a natural environment for producing both fast, radially stratified disc outflow and efficient disc angular-momentum removal. MAD-driven outflow may therefore provide a magnetic origin for ultra-fast absorbers in low-luminosity active galactic nuclei (AGNs) and X-ray binaries (XRBs).
#### Effects of Synchrotron Self-Absorption on Polarization Diagnostics of MHD Turbulence
 - **Authors:** Ning-Ning Guo, Jian-Fu Zhang, Hua-Ping Xiao, Qing-Zhong Liu
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33696

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33696

 - **Abstract**
 Synchrotron polarization is a powerful tool for diagnosing magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence, yet prior analyses are confined to the optically thin regime, neglecting the effects of synchrotron self-absorption (SSA). Here, we incorporate SSA into the radiative transfer of polarized synchrotron emission and employ 3D MHD turbulence simulations to investigate its impacts on polarization statistics. We show that SSA strongly modulates polarized intensity by suppressing its emission amplitude, steepening its low-frequency power-law slopes, and reducing scale-dependent anisotropy. Notably, SSA does not impede the recovery of the scaling slope of the magnetic turbulence. Our results extend the applicability of synchrotron polarization diagnostics to optically thick astrophysical environments, providing a theoretical foundation for interpreting multi-frequency radio polarization observations. This confirms that polarization statistical techniques remain effective for diagnosing MHD turbulence properties in astrophysical environments where SSA is present.
#### The Fate of Ice Sheets on Near-Synchronously Rotating Land Planets
 - **Authors:** Sarah Silverman, Thomas Navarro, Martin Turbet, Nicolas B. Cowan, Natalya Gomez, Siddharth Bhatnagar, Emeline Bolmont
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33884

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33884

 - **Abstract**
 Rocky exoplanets orbiting M dwarf stars are often assumed to be synchronously rotating, such that water-trapping in ice sheets on the permanent nightside may pose a threat to habitability. However, in compact M dwarf systems, gravitational interactions between planets may cause the substellar point to librate or circulate on millennial timescales and sporadically change direction or speed. Here, we use a 3D global climate model to quantify, for the first time, the water-trapping capabilities of these "near"-synchronously rotating exoplanets, with TRAPPIST-1e as our test case. Across a range of synodic day lengths, we find that there is a permanent polar ice reservoir and an increasingly large build-up of lower-latitude transient nightside ice for longer days. A persistent ice-free region develops on the dayside where surface conditions can sustain liquid water. However, this steady-state ice distribution may never be realized since the accumulation timescale may be slower than changes in the spin regime. Additionally, we revise the nightside ice build-up on a synchronously rotating exoplanet using a coupled atmosphere+ice numerical model that accounts for atmospheric lapse rate and isostatic adjustment feedbacks. We find that accounting for this ice-elevation feedback reduces the predicted ice accumulation by a factor of ~3. Taken together, our study suggests that water-trapping in ice sheets is less severe than previously thought for land planets orbiting M dwarf stars.

#### Halo formation and distribution around cosmic web
 - **Authors:** Hongfei Yu, Huiyuan Wang, Yangyao Chen, Ziwen Zhang, Hao Li
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34042

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34042

 - **Abstract**
 We use the ELUCID simulation to study how the local environment affects the formation of dark matter halos and their spatial distribution around the cosmic web. We describe the environment by local density and cosmic-web type. At fixed web type, the halo mass function (HMF) depends strongly on the local density, whereas at fixed density it depends only weakly, though significantly, on the web type; the character of both dependencies changes around the mean density. The halo formation redshift is primarily driven by local density rather than cosmic web. We then introduce the three-dimensional cosmic-web cross-correlation function (3D-WCCF), which expresses the distribution of halos around cosmic web in the local tidal eigenframe. The 3D-WCCF provides numerous anisotropic characteristics. Around all web types, halos are most strongly correlated along the axis of weakest compression, $\mathbf{e}_3$, and least along the axis of strongest compression, $\mathbf{e}_1$. Nearly all cluster-sized halos reside in knots and lie preferentially in the plane perpendicular to ${\bf e}_1$. The 3D-WCCF also provides important information on the geometry and size of filaments, sheets and voids. Moreover, early-forming halos are more strongly correlated with the web than late-forming ones, and this assembly bias is anisotropic around filaments, sheets, and voids, being strongest along $\mathbf{e}_3$, but nearly isotropic around knots.
