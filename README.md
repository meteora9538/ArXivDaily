#### Non-Axisymmetric Pillar Structures due to Embedded Stars in AGN Accretion Discs
 - **Authors:** Jiamu Huang, Douglas N.C. Lin, Keith Horne
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31751

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31751

 - **Abstract**
 Gravitationally unstable outer regions of AGN discs may host in situ formation of massive stars whose heating produces localized vertical "pillars" on the disc surface, in the same region that emits the broad lines. Here we investigate the reverberation-mapping signatures that such pillars would produce. We compute velocity-delay maps $\Psi(\lambda, \tau)$, time-evolving emission-line spectra, and continuum reverberation lag spectra for these structures. We model each pillar as a 2D Gaussian perturbation to the disc height under lamp-post irradiation and weight the line response with Cloudy photoionization grids covering the major BLR lines. We show H$\alpha$, Mg II, and C IV as representative examples. A single pillar produces a localized hotspot in $\Psi$ and a clear "S-wave" pattern in the time-evolving spectrum that persists, though diluted, for $N \sim 100$ pillars. Emission-line fluxes are suppressed in shadows behind the pillars, but when normalized by H$\beta$ (or by the local continuum) the line-to-H$\beta$ ratios move in opposite directions: Mg II/H$\beta$ rises while C IV/H$\beta$ drops. This contrast distinguishes pillars from purely kinematic features that would modulate all lines by similar factors. Across scans of the pillar shape and lamp-post height, the pillar models produce an optical continuum reverberation lag spectrum of the observed magnitude. The once-per-orbit modulation grows with $\sin i$ from foreshortening, and at sufficiently high inclination the pillar's body geometrically self-occults its lamp-facing inner slope, adding a sharper near/far contrast. These signatures provide observational diagnostics for detecting in situ star formation in AGN discs through velocity-resolved reverberation mapping campaigns.
#### An Extra-nuclear Water Maser in NGC 1106 Likely Driven By An AGN Shock: Implications for Maser-based Black Hole Mass Measurements
 - **Authors:** Macon Magno, Emmanuel Momjian, Krista L. Smith, O. Ivy Wong, Travis C. Fischer, Michael J. Koss, Chenoa D. Tremblay
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31753

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31753

 - **Abstract**
 We present Very Large Array A-configuration ($0.13$'') 22 GHz spectral-line and continuum observations of the Swift-BAT detected Seyfert 2 galaxy, NGC 1106, aimed at localizing a water maser line offset from the host systemic velocity by 541 km s$^{-1}$. The 22 GHz continuum emission reveals a 352 pc extended structure within the nucleus of NGC 1106, and we spatially resolve the water maser emission to a compact region $\sim1$ kpc ($3.12$'') from the nucleus. Comparing our data with previous 22 GHz 1'' observations, we found that the water maser lies near the boundary of a radio extension aligned with the jet axis, likely arising from the interface between the active galactic nucleus (AGN) outflow and the host galaxy's ISM. To verify this, we compared the water maser in NGC 1106 to a known water maser in a star-forming region within the Milky Way, along with known extragalactic water masers associated with star formation and AGN driven outflows. Overall, we found that the maser position, velocity offset, flux density, and alignment with the jet axis are consistent with an origin at the interface between the AGN outflow and the host ISM. Our results have important implications for maser-based black hole mass measurements in which disk geometry is ambiguous. Since single-dish observations cannot spatially separate disk masers from outflow-driven masers, high-velocity maser features attributed to the accretion disk may also include contributions from kpc-scale shocks.
#### Image Morphology of Misaligned Magnetically Arrested Disks
 - **Authors:** Samuel Feyan, Cora Prather, Dominic O. Chang, Daniel C. M. Palumbo, Koushik Chatterjee, Michael D. Johnson
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31754

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31754

 - **Abstract**
 Horizon scale images probe the plasma dynamics and spacetime in the vicinity of accreting black holes. Interpreting these images requires models that connect image morphology to the structure of the underlying accretion flow. Disk misalignment is expected in many systems, yet most horizon scale image analyses assume that the emitting flow is aligned with the black hole spin. We investigate whether tilted accretion flows produce image features distinguishable from axisymmetric flows and whether their effects can be absorbed by changes in aligned model parameters. Using general relativistic magnetohydrodynamic simulations of tilted magnetically arrested disks spanning black hole spins $a^*=-0.9375$ to $0.9375$ and initial disk tilts $0^\circ$ to $30^\circ$, we generate synthetic images and quantify their morphology through fits to an aligned, semi-analytic emission model. Using this approach, we show that high spin prograde models show little sensitivity to disk tilt, as the inner emitting flow aligns with the black hole spin. In contrast, retrograde tilted disks develop warped, non-axisymmetric inner structure that distorts the inner shadow and strongly alters image morphology. While the aligned semi-analytic model reproduces most prograde images well, retrograde tilted disk images are fit less accurately and yield biased inferred parameters, especially for spin. The image morphologies indicate that tilt affects horizon scale images only when misalignment survives into the near horizon emitting region, motivating image models that include disk tilt as an explicit degree of freedom when alignment is uncertain.
#### From Inspiral to Outburst: Modeling a Potential Luminous Red Nova from Betelgeuse
 - **Authors:** Naunet Leonhardes-Barboza, Andrea Antoni, Morgan MacLeod, Angela Twum, Enrico Ramirez-Ruiz
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31755

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31755

 - **Abstract**
 Recent evidence suggests that Betelgeuse may host a stellar companion whose orbit will decay through tidal interactions, ultimately leading to engulfment and merger within the next several thousand years. If confirmed, this system could represent the nearest and best-characterized progenitor of a future luminous red nova (LRN). We investigate whether the shock-breakout and cooling emission preceding the LRN can be used to constrain the properties of the companion and its disruption within the stellar envelope. Using binary stellar evolution calculations, we evolve Betelgeuse with companions of $0.7$, $1.0$, and $2.0 M_\odot$ to the onset of common envelope evolution (CEE). We then perform one-dimensional radiation-hydrodynamic simulations that follow companion disruption, energy deposition, and shock propagation through the envelope. We find that the peak luminosity increases systematically with companion mass, from $2.44\times10^{41}$ to $6.17\times10^{41}$ erg s$^{-1}$ across the $0.7$-$2.0 M_\odot$ models, while the duration increases more modestly from $1.48$ to $1.99$ days and attains apparent magnitudes brighter than Venus as viewed from Earth. Tidal disruption contributes both kinetic energy and mass to the envelope, linking the companion's structure and mass to the energetics of the shock-heated ejecta. The monotonic luminosity sequence demonstrates that the early breakout emission can retain direct information about the engulfed companion. The interaction ejects $6.15$-$11.19 M_\odot$ of hydrogen-rich material, substantially modifying both the envelope structure and circumstellar environment prior to core collapse.
#### Cluster connectivity in DES Y3 data: impact on galaxy properties
 - **Authors:** C. Gouin, C. Laigle, J. Prat, L. O.V. Edwards, P. Ganeshaiah Veena, D. Galárraga-Espinosa, G. Gopidas
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31800

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31800

 - **Abstract**
 Connectivity, namely the number of cosmic filaments reaching out of groups or clusters, has recently emerged as an informative way to quantify environment. With the advent of wide-area photometric surveys, it becomes possible to measure connectivity on a statistically significant sample of clusters. In this work, we extract the cosmic web from the DES Y3 photometric data and measure the connectivity around clusters from several public catalogs up to $z < 1$. Cosmic filaments are extracted in 2D with DisPerSE (the parametrization of which is calibrated on simulations) in tomographic slices of fixed comoving thickness of 220 Mpc, with a constant sampling across the redshift range ($\sim 0.1$ galaxies Mpc$^{-2}$). We first recover the well-established trend that cluster connectivity increases with cluster mass at all redshifts. We then investigate the properties of cluster member galaxies and find that, at fixed mass, clusters with a higher fraction of red (elliptical) galaxies are more strongly connected to the cosmic web. Furthermore, by examining the radial profiles of the red and elliptical galaxy fractions, we obtain hints that galaxy populations may undergo pre-processing within filamentary structures before being accreted into clusters. Overall, we demonstrate that despite photometric redshift uncertainties and projection effects, our method allows for the quantification of the cosmic web environment around clusters in broad-band photometric surveys. This work is consistent with results based on the Euclid Quick Release 1 (Q1). We plan to publicly release the cluster connectivity catalog as well as the cosmic filament catalog.
#### Tilt, warp, and dissipation flare in hydrodynamic disks around kicked compact objects
 - **Authors:** Yoonsoo Kim
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31990

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31990

 - **Abstract**
 We study the dynamical response of a hydrodynamic disk to an impulsive kick imparted to a central massive object, focusing on the global properties of the material that remains gravitationally bound. We construct a simple analytic model and show that the bound material can acquire a significant tilt---or even a retrograde rotation---if the kick is directed close to the disk midplane. We perform 3D numerical simulations and show that (1) the bound material relaxes to a torus-like circularized flow of which total mass and angular momentum is in fair agreement with the analytic estimate, and (2) in spite of nonlinear hydrodynamic effects, a highly tilted or retrograde disk can be sufficiently formed. The simulations additionally reveal a transient warp in the kicked disk, suggesting a kick as a formation mechanism of tilted or warped disks around compact objects. About 10% of the kinetic energy the bound material acquired from the kick is converted to the internal energy, while the exact conversion efficiency depends on the kick angle and the initial surface mass density profile. The luminosity of this kick-driven dissipation flare can reach $\sim 10^{43}\,{\rm erg\,s^{-1}}$ for strong kicks ($V_{\rm kick} \sim 1000$km/s). We discuss the implications of our results for recoiling supermassive black holes, recoiled stellar-mass black holes in the disks of active galactic nuclei, and natal kicks on compact objects.

#### Star clusters at cosmic dawn I: the emergence of the initial cluster mass function
 - **Authors:** Fred S. Thompson, Julien Devriendt, Adrianne Slyz, Taysun Kimm, Cheonsu Kang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32497

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32497

 - **Abstract**
 JWST observations of lensed galaxies reveal unusually bright star-cluster candidates within the first billion years after the Big Bang. However, the physical processes shaping the initial cluster mass function (ICMF) remain uncertain. We study the formation of a galaxy embedded in a dark matter halo whose virial mass reaches $10^{10} \mathrm{M}_\odot$ at $z=6$, using cosmological radiation-hydrodynamics zoom-in simulations. Its star clusters, represented by sink particles, accrete gas and form stellar populations that supply radiation and supernova feedback, thereby regulating their own growth. As we do not prescribe an efficiency per free-fall time or a fixed star-cluster formation duration, the ICMF emerges self-consistently. We assess the effects of numerical resolution and stellar radiation. Schechter fits to the central-galaxy ICMF give truncation masses near $6 \times 10^6 \mathrm{M}_\odot$, with substantial uncertainty. At fixed resolution, including stellar radiation makes the low-mass slope of the ICMF shallower, from $-1.60$ to $-1.34$, while doubling the spatial resolution steepens it back to $-1.54$. Stellar radiation also reduces the abundance of low-mass clouds, particularly those with lower gas densities, and shortens the formation time of star clusters to about a Myr, i.e. well before their first supernovae go off. On average, more rapidly fed star clusters accrete for longer, making their final masses rise faster than linearly with the initial accretion rate and contributing to a flatter ICMF. At fixed host-galaxy star formation rate, our brightest simulated clusters reach UV luminosities comparable to those of observed lensed star-cluster candidates.

In this work, we model the population of binary black holes using generic subpopulations with distinct mass-ratio, spin-magnitude, and spin-tilt distributions, whose relative contributions vary with primary mass. Using the 259 binary black holes in the fifth LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA catalog (GWTC-5), we resolve three subpopulations with low, medium, and high spins. The low-spin population dominates for primary masses near $35 M_\odot$, contributes roughly half of the binaries for primary masses of $10 M_\odot$, and favors nearly equal mass ratios overall; its spins are consistent with both black holes having negligible spin, with a log Bayes factor of $\log_{10}B =6.1^{+0.2}_{-0.3}$ in favor of vanishing spins. The medium-spin population accounts for the other half of the population around primary masses of $10 M_\odot$ and favors spin magnitudes $\chi_i \approx 0.2$ for both components, preferentially aligned orientations, and comparable mass ratios. The high-spin population dominates above primary masses of $60 M_\odot$ and also contributes at lower masses $\sim 20 M_\odot$; it favors a rapidly spinning primary ($\chi_1 \approx 0.7$), a broad range of secondary spins, widely distributed spin orientations, and a broad mass-ratio distribution. Allowing for a fourth subpopulation yields no distinct additional mass-ratio or spin distribution, as it becomes degenerate with existing features. To resolve highly concentrated features in spin, we control the variance of standard Monte Carlo likelihood estimators using truncated Gaussian mixture models. Taken together, our results add to a growing body of evidence for substructure in the population of LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA black holes, providing further clues to uncover their formation histories.
