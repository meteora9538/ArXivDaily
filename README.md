#### The Local Star Formation Environment Predicts Young Star Cluster Radii: Evidence from PHANGS-MUSE H-alpha Maps
 - **Authors:** Michelle Sebastian
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23876

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23876

 - **Abstract**
 The effective radii of young star clusters correlate with cluster mass, but the large scatter about that relation is not well understood. Theory predicts that a cluster's initial radius depends on the gas surface density of its natal environment, so the star formation environment should leave a measurable imprint on cluster structure. In a companion analysis of 5,105 clusters across 31 LEGUS galaxies, we found that galaxy-wide specific star formation rate (sSFR) carries no predictive power for cluster radius. Here we test whether that null result reflects the spatial scale on which the environment was measured. We cross-match the LEGUS cluster catalog of Brown & Gnedin with H-alpha maps from the PHANGS-MUSE survey in the four galaxies common to both surveys, and measure the local star formation rate surface density, Sigma_SFR, at the position of every cluster by aperture photometry. For the 325 clusters younger than 10 Myr with reliable radius and mass measurements, local Sigma_SFR significantly predicts effective radius (beta = 0.079, p = 2 x 10^-4) in a model containing mass and age, while the host galaxy's sSFR does not (beta = 0.042, p = 0.40) for those same clusters. The local model generalizes nearly three times better under cross-validation (CV R^2 = 0.061 versus 0.022). The effect operates among clusters within a single galaxy: it survives galaxy fixed effects (beta = 0.068, p = 0.001) and appears independently in each of the two galaxies large enough to fit alone. Including local Sigma_SFR also reduces the mass coefficient by 26%. The birth environment measurably shapes cluster structure, but only when it is measured on the scale on which clusters actually form.
#### Environmental dependence in the halo model informed by the FLAMINGO simulations
 - **Authors:** G. Martín Girardi, D. Rana, H. Hoekstra, J. Schaye, M. Schaller
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23997

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23997

 - **Abstract**
 Galaxy-galaxy lensing (GGL) measurements around galaxy samples in different environments provide valuable information about the galaxy-halo connection. In such studies the halo model is commonly used to estimate physical parameters from the lensing signal, but the impact of environment is generally not considered. Our aim is to adapt the halo model to allow a density-split GGL analysis and to understand the environmental dependence of the elements of the model. We use the FLAMINGO suite of cosmological hydrodynamical simulations to construct samples of halos in underdense and overdense regions. Environment density is assigned to halos based on halo counts within an aperture, employing different aperture sizes to probe both local and large scales. We utilize spherical apertures with radii between $1 h^{-1} {\rm Mpc}$ and $10 h^{-1} {\rm Mpc}$. We find a strong dependence on environment for the halo mass function (HMF) and the halo bias. To account for this, we propose an empirical modification that can be applied to existing HMF prescriptions. We find that after a density split, the halo bias is approximately constant with mass. There is also an environmental dependence for the stellar-to-halo-mass relation (SHMR), the conditional stellar mass function, and the mass-concentration relation, all of which can be addressed with existing models. We evaluate the impact of overlooking environment on halo occupation distribution (HOD) parameter inference, finding that it leads to biased estimations of the SHMR and the satellite fraction. These results emphasize the need to review the assumptions adopted in the halo model when working with samples of galaxies binned by environment, and consequently, by galaxy properties that correlate with environment, such as morphology and color.
#### Consistently Reproducing Early Galaxy Observations and 21-cm Signal during the Epoch of Reionization
 - **Authors:** Michele Bianco, Sambit K. Giri, Arghyadeep Basu\orcidlink, Tommaso Boschi, Aurel Schneider, Enrico Garaldi, Garrelt Mellema
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24064

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24064

 - **Abstract**
 In the standard cosmological model, galaxies forming within dark matter haloes are the primary drivers of the Epoch of Reionization (EoR). The complex physics of galaxy formation introduces scatter in the relation between halo mass and star-formation activity, with implications for the production and escape of ionising photons. In this work, we implement a stochastic source model in our cosmological radiative transfer simulations. We calibrate our model against the high-redshift ultraviolet luminosity function observed by JWST and observational constraints on Lyman-continuum leakage to constrain the ionising escape fraction. We find that stochastic star formation redistributes ionising emissivity toward lower-mass haloes, producing a more fragmented ionisation topology and delaying the growth of the largest ionised regions relative to the deterministic, observationally calibrated model. However, when the two models match total ionising photon production, their global neutral fractions and 21-cm power spectrum remain nearly indistinguishable, despite small differences in the morphology of individual ionised regions. Additionally, we establish a unified framework that lets us apply source models derived from computationally expensive hydrodynamical simulations (SPICE, FIRE-2 and Thesan) and investigate their impact on large-volume reionisation simulations, extending their prescriptions to scales of >100 Mpc. We find that these prescriptions produce distinct ionisation topologies and 21-cm statistical signatures, even when their global reionisation histories are similar. Our results highlight the importance of combining physically motivated galaxy formation prescriptions from hydrodynamical simulations with large-volume radiative transfer simulations to interpret forthcoming 21-cm observations and constrain the sources of cosmic reionisation.

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24097

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24097

 - **Abstract**
 We present a methodology for analysing radio sources in galaxy clusters by combining multi-frequency radio observations, X-ray analysis of the intracluster medium (ICM), and three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulations, and apply it to the galaxy cluster RXCJ1454.4+1622 and its associated FR II radio galaxy 3C 306 (IC 4516). The radio emission exhibits a curved structure connecting the lobes, likely indicating a complex dynamical evolution influenced by environmental effects. Spectral index and break-frequency maps reveal a relatively homogeneous radiative ageing distribution, with characteristic spectral ages of $\sim220$ Myr and a smooth spatial gradient. From equipartition arguments, we estimate an equivalent jet magnetic field of $2.1 \pm 0.3\,\mu$G, consistent with values observed in FR II radio galaxies. These observational constraints are used to initialise non-relativistic MHD simulations exploring three scenarios: jet precession, bulk motion of the source through the ICM, and a combined model including both effects. Neither pure precession nor pure source motion alone fully reproduces the observed morphology, whereas the combined model provides a more consistent representation of the jet curvature and lobe asymmetries. Nevertheless, the simulations indicate that source motion is the dominant mechanism shaping the large-scale radio morphology, with jet precession playing a secondary role. The inferred source velocity ($\sim280$ km s$^{-1}$) and precession timescale ($\sim250$ Myr) are compatible with the spectral ageing constraints. These results suggest that the morphology of 3C 306 is influenced by the interplay between jet precession and dynamical interaction with the ICM, highlighting the role of environmental effects in radio-galaxy evolution in clusters.
#### Critical magnetic Reynolds number of the weakly-collisional turbulent dynamo in supersonic plasmas
 - **Authors:** Radhika Achikanath Chirakkara, Christoph Federrath
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Plasma Physics (physics.plasm-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24394

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24394

 - **Abstract**
 Weakly-collisional plasmas are found in several turbulent astrophysical environments, such as the Intra-galaxy-cluster medium (ICM) and the solar wind, where magnetic fields can be amplified by the turbulent dynamo. The onset of dynamo action is determined by the critical magnetic Reynolds number, ${\rm Rm}_{\rm crit}$, above which turbulent motions amplify magnetic energy. Although ${\rm Rm}_{\rm crit}$ has been studied extensively in collisional magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence, its value in supersonic weakly-collisional plasmas remains unknown. Here we determine ${\rm Rm}_{\rm crit}$ using three-dimensional hybrid particle-in-cell (HPIC) simulations of solenoidally driven turbulence with a sonic Mach number $\approx 2$. We vary the magnetic Reynolds number over ${\rm Rm}\approx31$-$1000$ and consider two initial plasma magnetisations, characterised by initial Larmor ratios $r_{\rm Larmor, 0}/L_{\rm box}=1$ and $100$. We find that the magnetic energy grows exponentially at sufficiently large ${\rm Rm}$ and decays when magnetic diffusion dominates at low ${\rm Rm}$. By fitting the magnetic-energy growth rate as a function of ${\rm Rm}$, we measure ${\rm Rm}_{\rm crit}=190^{+21}_{-17}$ and $198^{+16}_{-18}$ for the initially magnetised and unmagnetised plasma, respectively. These values are the same within their uncertainties, indicating that the initial plasma magnetisation does not significantly affect ${\rm Rm}_{\rm crit}$ in the supersonic regime. The measured values are higher than those previously obtained for the subsonic weakly-collisional dynamo, showing that increasing compressibility makes weakly-collisional turbulent dynamo action more difficult to excite. The measured values are also approximately 1.5 times higher than the ${\rm Rm}_{\rm crit}$ value reported for supersonic MHD turbulence.
#### Statistical Strong Lensing V. Inference with Microlensed Quasars
 - **Authors:** Shuo Zhang, Alessandro Sonnenfeld
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24423

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24423

 - **Abstract**
 Microlensing of strongly lensed quasars provides direct information on the stellar component of lens galaxies, and can help break the degeneracy between the stellar initial mass function (IMF) and the dark matter. However, a statistical framework to forward model microlensing data while accounting for strong lensing selection effects is still missing. We incorporated microlensing effects into the hierarchical model of Paper III, and investigated to what extent flux measurements of strongly lensed quasars can break the degeneracy between the stellar IMF and the dark matter distribution. We tested the method on a mock sample of 219 doubly imaged quasars, lensed by circularly symmetric lenses. When microlensing effects are modelled, the inference using image fluxes recovers the input foreground population parameters and provides tighter constraints than the inference without flux constraints. The improvement is clearest in the $\mu_\alpha-\mu_5$ plane, where $\mu_\alpha$ describes the mean stellar population synthesis mismatch parameter $\log\alpha_{\rm SPS}$ and $\mu_5$ describes the mean projected dark matter mass within $5\,{\rm kpc}$. The 68% credible interval on $\mu_\alpha$ shrinks from 0.16 dex to 0.09 dex, with the degeneracy between $\mu_\alpha$ and $\mu_5$ significantly reduced. By contrast, fitting the image fluxes without accounting for microlensing leads to large biases on the inferred parameters. This work is a first step towards an accurate modelling of microlensing data. Application to real lens samples will require dropping the circular symmetry assumption and adopting more realistic models for the microlensing magnification.
#### The Impact of an AGN on PAH Spectral Features in Galaxy Evolution Simulations: Continuum Dilution and Excitation of PAHs
 - **Authors:** Grant P. Donnelly, Desika Narayanan, Paul Torrey, J. -D. T. Smith, Helena M. Richie, Hui Li, Federico Marinacci
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24778

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24778

 - **Abstract**
 Galaxies hosting an active galactic nucleus (AGN) show diminished power and non-standard spectral feature ratios from polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) compared with normal star-forming galaxies. Proposed mechanisms for this difference include a combination of AGN-driven PAH grain processing, differential heating from the surrounding radiation field, and continuum dilution. These potentially competing factors are difficult to disentangle observationally. In this paper, we investigate the impact of the AGN radiation field on the excitation properties and potential dilution of the mid-IR emission features via constrained numerical experiments of idealized galaxies in simulations that include a self-consistent model for the evolution of dust. We find that, for the integrated spectrum of our simulated Milky Way analog and independent of grain-processing physics, the apparent PAH emission is progressively diminished as AGN luminosity is increased. For spectra within the central half kiloparsec, PAH features are entirely obfuscated when an AGN of any sampled luminosity is present. We additionally find that the UV radiation from an AGN is capable of impacting PAH band ratios, though note that we do not yet include a model for potential photodestruction of PAHs. Our model suggests that the inferred lack of PAHs via featureless mid-IR spectra from galaxies hosting AGN may simply owe to the direct continuum from the AGN overpowering mid-IR features from PAHs in the ISM of the host galaxy. Future work will investigate the relative role of AGN continuum dilution and PAH heating vs photodestruction/ionization of PAHs.

 - **Abstract**
 We present the chemical-abundance distributions and star-formation histories of the M31 halo components using photometric Fe and $\alpha$ abundance estimates derived from the PAndAS broad-band $g$ and $i$ filters and the Subaru/HSC $NB515$ narrow-band filter. We selected about 76,400 candidate M31 giant stars and constructed a synthetic photometric training dataset based on model isochrones. #### Simulations of Particle Acceleration by an Interplanetary Shock Observed at 0.07 au and 0.7 au
 - **Authors:** F.-J. Kong, G. Qin, Y. Wang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24995

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24995

 - **Abstract**
 Parker Solar Probe (PSP) observed a coronal mass ejection (CME)-driven shock at 0.07 au on 2022 September 5, and the same CME-driven interplanetary shock was subsequently observed by Solar Orbiter (SolO) at 0.7 au on 2022 September 6. Such a large difference in heliocentric distance between the two shock events enables our comparative study of particle acceleration in near-Sun and far-Sun regions. Using a backward-in-time test-particle approach in a slab-2D turbulent magnetic field model, we perform particle acceleration simulations for the 0.07 au PSP shock and 0.7 au SolO shock, covering the energy ranges measured by PSP/EPI-Lo (0.0697-8.06 MeV) and SolO/EPD (0.0495-5.83 MeV). With an initial kappa = 3 kappa distribution, the particle fluxes at the shock passage are successfully reproduced using effective local shock angles of 70 degrees for the PSP shock and 80 degrees for the SolO shock. Both the observed and simulated energy spectra exhibit a double power-law form. The 0.07 au PSP spectrum softens above 0.75 MeV, while the 0.7 au SolO spectrum hardens slightly above 0.51 MeV, revealing distinct near-Sun and far-Sun acceleration environments. Compared with the observations, the simulations yield harder energy spectra with higher break energies of 2 MeV at 0.07 au and 3 MeV at 0.7 au, indicating efficient particle acceleration under the initial kappa = 3 distribution. Both the observed and simulated spectral indices are harder than the standard diffusive shock acceleration (DSA) prediction, likely arising from non-local acceleration at the large-angle quasi-perpendicular shocks, efficient injection of pre-accelerated suprathermal particles, and the finite shock lifetime effect.
#### IMF-aware N-body forward modelling of membership selection functions for young open clusters
 - **Authors:** Yue-Yue Shen, Chao Liu, Zepeng Zheng, Long Wang, Xiaoying Pang, Bo Zhang, Hao Tian, Yang Xiang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25200

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25200

 - **Abstract**
 We present an IMF-aware N-body forward-modelling framework, in which the stellar initial mass function (IMF) is an explicit input, to quantify the membership selection functions of open clusters, namely completeness and contamination as functions of magnitude. Accurate derivation of the IMF requires correcting for these selection effects, which otherwise distort the inferred present-day mass function. Our framework simulates clusters with IMF slopes $\alpha_2 = -2.7, -2.3, -1.9$, converts them into synthetic Gaia observations, adds mock field populations, and recovers members with HDBSCAN to mimic observational membership selection. Applying this framework to 35 young open clusters (ages $\lesssim100$ Myr, distances $\lesssim600$ pc), we find declining completeness towards the faint limit; older, more expanded, and more diffuse models have lower completeness and higher contamination. For a given cluster, models with flatter IMFs yield modestly but systematically lower global completeness and higher contamination, despite initial cluster masses calibrated to the same observed member count. These models undergo more stellar evolution mass loss and expand more, resulting in lower projected stellar densities; their lower completeness is consistent with more diffuse clusters being harder to separate from field stars. Corrections derived from a mismatched IMF largely recover the total star counts but leave a larger distortion in the shape of the present-day luminosity function than those derived with the correct IMF. Because membership recovery is tied to a cluster's structural evolution and the specific membership algorithm used, our findings emphasize that selection functions should be inferred jointly with the IMF rather than assumed in advance.

 - **Abstract**

 - **Abstract**
 We present the first local simulations of accretion disc breaking (or disc tearing) with differential precession. Precession can occur due to a non-spherical potential (e.g. tilted circumbinary discs), or relativistic effects (e.g. tilted discs around spinning black holes).
