#### Chemical abundance patterns at z=1-7: Carbon, Nitrogen, Neon, Sulphur, and Argon abundances from JWST spectroscopy

Ceci, A. Chakraborty, Q. D'Amato, C. Kobayashi, C. Marconcini, R. Maiolino, L. Querci, M. Scialpi, L. Ulivi, G. Venturi, M. V. Zanchettin
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28717

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28717

 - **Abstract**
 The relative abundances of chemical elements in the interstellar medium (ISM) trace the history of star formation and chemical enrichment in galaxies. We investigate the abundance patterns of carbon (C), nitrogen (N), neon (Ne), sulfur (S), and argon (Ar) in star-forming galaxies at z=1-7, combining ultra-deep JWST/NIRSpec observations from the MARTA survey with archival spectroscopy. We assemble 244 galaxies with [OIII]4363 detections and derive direct Te-based abundances. New empirical strong-line calibrations for N/O, Ne/O, S/O, and Ar/O extend the analysis to an additional 514 galaxies. We find distinct abundance-metallicity trends. N/O is enhanced by 0.18 dex in median relative to local galaxies, with a stronger enhancement at low metallicity. Direct measurements reveal decreasing S/O and Ar/O with increasing metallicity, while strong-line measurements suggest a flattening at higher metallicities, especially for S/O. We also identify a stellar-mass dependence in the O/Ar-Ar/H plane, with lower-mass galaxies exhibiting lower O/Ar at fixed Ar/H, consistent with differences in star-formation efficiency and delayed SNe Ia enrichment. Ne/O remains approximately constant up to 12+log(O/H)=8.2, with a mild increase at higher metallicities and no significant redshift evolution. C/O tentatively increases with metallicity, broadly following the local relation. Finally, a forward model shows that observational selection affects the abundance ratios differently: it mainly restricts the accessible metallicity range for Ne/O and Ar/O, but preferentially selects galaxies with enhanced N/O at low metallicity, with a weaker effect for S/O. These results provide new constraints on chemical enrichment at high redshift and highlight the importance of accounting for selection effects when interpreting abundance-metallicity trends.
#### The Balmer Absorber Strength Problem of Little Red Dots
 - **Authors:** Hanpu Liu, Eliot Quataert, Bingjie Wang, Jenny E. Greene, Yan-Fei Jiang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28720

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28720

 - **Abstract**
 Little Red Dots (LRDs) commonly show Balmer absorption features. Some observed LRD spectra have weaker absorption in H$\alpha$ than in H$\beta$, in apparent contradiction to atomic physics according to standard attenuation models. This tension is naturally resolved when one considers emission from the absorbing medium itself, which is ignored in the standard model: when the Balmer absorption is optically thick, a non-negligible source function could fill in the H$\alpha$ absorption trough and reduce its observed absorption equivalent width (EW). With proof-of-concept photoionization models we show that the source function of H$\alpha$ could be systematically stronger than that of H$\beta$. This is due to radiative trapping preferentially populating the $n=3$ level of neutral hydrogen, which operates efficiently when the medium is optically thick to H$\alpha$ but not very optically thick to Paschen-$\alpha$. Such line-dependent source functions could lead to significant corrections to the expected EW and selectively weaken H$\alpha$ absorption, consistent with observations. Quantitatively, this depends on the distance of the absorbing medium and its internal structure, providing a new diagnostic of the environment around LRDs. Future modeling of the absorbers may also potentially open a new avenue for estimating the mass of the central engine.
#### Arepo-CD: Coupling Collisional $N$-Body Dynamics with Moving-Mesh Magnetohydrodynamics
 - **Authors:** Chuizheng Kong, Hui Li, Long Wang, Yunwei Deng, Elizabeth Moreno-Hilario
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28727

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28727

 - **Abstract**
 Star formation is a hierarchical, clustered process in turbulent giant molecular clouds (GMCs), where close stellar encounters can strongly affect cluster evolution and feedback. However, gravitational softening in many hydrodynamic simulations of GMCs limits the treatment of these encounters. We present Arepo-CD (Collisional Dynamics), a framework that couples star-by-star collisional $N$-body dynamics to the moving-mesh magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) code Arepo. Star-star interactions use unsoftened gravity, while interactions involving gas or collisionless components remain softened. Compact stellar subsystems are identified on the fly and evolved with a fourth-order forward symplectic integrator and slow-down algorithmic regularization coupled to the native Arepo timestep hierarchy. Benchmark tests against the collisional $N$-body code PeTar show good agreement in cluster core collapse and subsequent expansion, radial density profiles, and stellar velocity distributions. Coupled to the Rigel star formation and feedback model, Arepo-CD follows cluster formation and early evolution in radiation MHD simulations of a $10^5~M_\odot$ turbulent molecular cloud. In the fiducial GMC comparison, the collisional and softened-gravity runs yield similar star formation histories and integrated star formation efficiencies. Nevertheless, close gravitational encounters during the embedded phase generate a substantial runaway population, with a velocity tail extending beyond 100 km/s. Ejected massive stars redistribute radiative feedback into the surrounding lower-density medium and produce a more extended distribution of heated gas. Arepo-CD provides a practical and computationally efficient framework for studying collisional stellar dynamics and its coupling to gas evolution and feedback during star cluster formation in realistic galactic environments.

 - **Abstract**
 The source PSR J0901-4046 shows a very long rotational period, of $\approx 75.88$ s. In fact, its long period suggests that it could be a white dwarf. We examine the properties of the source and compare the outcomes under the hypotheses of the source being a highly magnetized white dwarf (B-WD) and neutron star. We find that assuming it to be a B-WD with a surface field $\sim 10^{10}$ G explains many properties, including its storage/rotational energy, explaining luminosity. We argue that some rotation-powered pulsars with extreme properties may possibly be white dwarfs rather than neutron stars.

 - **Abstract**
 The UV 2175 Å attenuation bump is one of the most prominent yet poorly understood features of galaxy attenuation curves. Its main candidate carrier is polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) molecules, which are more prevalent in high-metallicity environments. We present the first systematic study of the relationship between PAH abundance and the UV bump in 49 galaxies at cosmic noon (1.5 < z < 2.7), examining its dependence on stellar mass, star formation rate (SFR), and V-band optical depth. For 19 galaxies, we also investigate gas-phase metallicity using oxygen abundances. We measure the bump amplitude from VLT/MUSE spectroscopy and derive galaxy properties, including the PAH-to-dust mass fraction $q_{\rm PAH}$, through spectral energy distribution fitting with Prospector, combining JWST/NIRCam and JWST/MIRI photometry. Oxygen abundances are derived from strong-line diagnostics using Keck/MOSFIRE and JWST/NIRSpec data. We find a positive correlation between bump amplitude $E_{\rm bump}$ and $q_{\rm PAH}$ (Spearman $\rho = 0.60$). UV bumps are rarely detected at low $q_{\rm PAH}$, supporting PAHs as their primary carriers. Both $E_{\rm bump}$ and $q_{\rm PAH}$ correlate with stellar mass, oxygen abundance, and optical depth, and anticorrelate with specific SFR, suggesting that PAHs and the UV bump are suppressed in low-metallicity, dust-poor, and/or intensely star-forming environments. This is consistent with PAH destruction in environments with limited dust shielding and/or strong radiation fields. A principal component analysis of $E_{\rm bump}$, $q_{\rm PAH}$, optical depth, specific SFR, and oxygen abundance explains 97% of the variance, suggesting that previously reported correlations with stellar mass reflect an underlying dependence on metallicity. Finally, variations in dust geometry may explain the wide range of bump amplitudes (0-0.5 mag) at high $q_{\rm PAH}$.

 - **Abstract**
 We perform a multiple velocity component analysis of ammonia (NH$_3$) gas from KEYSTONE, a Green Bank Telescope survey covering 11 giant molecular clouds (GMCs) at distances from 0.7 to 2.7 kpc. We extend existing codes to enable simultaneous fitting of up to three velocity components in NH$_3$ (1,1) with radiative transfer. Using our multiple component fitting technique, we produce the best maps of the centroid velocity and velocity dispersion in these clouds to date. Over the entire survey, multiple velocity components were seen in 13% of pixels where we detect and fit NH$_3$ emission, primarily in clusters at the heart of the GMCs. Disentangling these velocity features will allow future quantitative analysis of coherent motions, such as infall and outflow, in these complex environments. #### Link between the properties of circumgalactic outflows and the recent star formation history of their host galaxies
 - **Authors:** Ismael Pessa, Lutz Wisotzki, Constanza Muñoz López, Tanya Urrutia, Ramona Augustin, Thierry Contini, Yucheng Guo, Daria Kozlova, Haruka Kusakabe, Sowgat Muzahid, John Pharo
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28752

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28752

 - **Abstract**
 Galactic outflows play a key role in galaxy evolution, yet their connection to the star formation history of their host galaxies remains poorly constrained. Building on previous work modeling extended Mg II emission of galaxies observed with VLT/MUSE to infer outflow properties, we investigate how these properties depend on the recent SFH. We derive non-parametric SFHs by fitting spectroscopic and photometric data with linear combinations of single stellar population models, and compute star formation rates over different timescales sensitive to recent stochastic bursts. We find moderate correlations between these SFRs (and sSFRs) and the kinematic properties of the outflows, namely maximum velocity and radial acceleration rate. Such correlations are not recovered using SFR tracers based on [O II] luminosity or parametric SFHs, highlighting the importance of capturing bursty star formation on timescales consistent with the traveling time of ejected gas into the circumgalactic medium. Using the extent and kinematics of the Mg II halos, we estimate each galaxy's outflow launching time and compute the SFR averaged over the relevant timescale. We identify a correlation between this SFR and the maximum outflow velocity, although it is not significantly stronger than those obtained using fixed averaging timescales. This suggests that the properties of Mg II halos are shaped by a series of star formation events rather than by a single dominant burst. Lastly, we find faster outflows in galaxies whose recent star formation is dominated by one or a few concentrated episodes, whereas galaxies with star formation spread more evenly over time exhibit slower outflows. These results indicate that the circumgalactic medium acts as a dynamical archive of recent star formation, linking bursty galaxy growth to outflow kinematics and providing a direct observational constraint on feedback models.

 - **Authors:** V. A. Capa, H. Dénes, K. A. Lutz, T. H. Brown

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28970

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28970

 - **Abstract**
 Galaxies with extreme amounts of neutral atomic hydrogen (HI), known as HI-eXtreme (HIX) galaxies, paradoxically exhibit normal or low star formation rates. To investigate the bottleneck in their star formation, we present ALMA CO(1-0) observations for five HIX survey galaxies: ESO208-G026, ESO378-G003, ESO381-G005, ESO417-G018, and ESO055-G013. While these systems possess massive, extended HI discs, their molecular gas discs are compact with normal H$_{2}$ masses relative to their stellar mass. Dynamical modelling with 3DBarolo reveals that the HI and molecular gas are kinematically consistent. This stability suppresses the atomic-to-molecular gas conversion, as evidenced by extremely long HI depletion times contrasted with typical H$_{2}$ depletion times. The high angular momentum and HI excess likely originate from past gas accretion or recent tidal interactions; this is especially apparent in ESO378-G003 and ESO381-G005, which exhibit extended HI structures and positive tidal indices.

The turnover in size at $M_\star\simeq5\times10^9\,M_\odot$ coincides with rapid growth of rotational support to $v/\sigma\simeq1.5$-$3$ in star-forming gas and stars younger than 10Myr, as the central circular speed exceeds the halo virial speed, which the star-forming gas velocity dispersion tracks.  - **Abstract**
 Aims: This study aims to place constraints on the spin of Sgr A* from recent flare observations. It models the flare kinematics in the vicinity of a supermassive black hole, evaluates their dependence on black hole spin, and assesses the correlation between the spin of the black hole and the astrometric observations in the Galactic center.
