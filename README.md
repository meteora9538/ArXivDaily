#### Where Cluster Galaxies Reside and How Strongly They Lens
 - **Authors:** Isaque Dutra, Priyamvada Natarajan, Cian Roche
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14167

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14167

 - **Abstract**
 The efficiency of galaxy-galaxy strong lensing (GGSL) in clusters depends jointly on the inner mass distributions of member galaxies and their locations within the cluster potential. Observed GGSL probabilities exceed $\Lambda$CDM predictions by nearly an order of magnitude, but the relative roles of internal structure and environment remain unclear. We disentangle these effects by comparing HST strong-lensing clusters with TNG-Cluster analogs at similar lens redshifts, matched by their primary Einstein radius. For each member, we measure its lensing strength in isolation and when embedded in the cluster field. Members in cluster-critical regions are classified as Major or Minor Perturbers depending on whether they strongly lens in isolation; those in subcritical regions are classified as Intrinsic Lenses, Boosted Lenses, or Inert Members according to whether they lens independently, only with environmental assistance, or not at all. Observed clusters contain 33.7% of selected members in cluster-critical regions, compared with 21.6% in TNG-Cluster, while their Major-to-Minor ratios are similar. Outside these regions, observations contain substantially more Intrinsic and Boosted Lenses and fewer Inert Members, with Boosted Lens fractions of 25.8% and 13.5%, respectively. The discrepancy persists at fixed subhalo mass and after including line-of-sight structure. A mass-conserving contraction mimicking core-collapsed self-interacting dark matter largely recovers the conditional lensing fractions but not cluster-critical occupancy. The GGSL discrepancy therefore reflects both insufficient central concentration in simulated members and differences in their locations within the cluster lensing field.
#### Stellar discs and intermediate-mass black holes in galactic nuclei II. Fragmenting the disc and forming the S-stars in a live nuclear star cluster
 - **Authors:** Taras Panamarev, Bence Kocsis
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14179

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14179

 - **Abstract**
 We present direct $N$-body simulations of a stellar disc around the supermassive black hole Sgr~A*, perturbed by an intermediate-mass black hole (IMBH) and embedded in a live nuclear star cluster. A retrograde IMBH with $m_\bullet=2000\,M_\odot$, relative inclination $\theta_\bullet=150^\circ$, and initial semi-major axis $a_\bullet=0.15$~pc breaks the disc into three disc-like components and an angular momentum filament. The stochastic torques of the cluster, together with the IMBH's inward migration, increase the angular separation between these substructures. By ${\sim}5$~Myr, their separations become comparable to those among the observed Galactic-centre young star structures. The innermost ($a\le0.04$~pc) disc stars evolve into an S-star-like population. Relaxation driven by the cluster disperses their orbital planes, excites their eccentricities and depletes the `zone of avoidance' within ${\sim}10$~Myr even without an IMBH. The IMBH further accelerates their orbital-plane reorientation. However, the most eccentric S-stars may require an additional formation channel, such as the Hills mechanism. The distinctive signatures of the IMBH are the well-separated disc components and the eccentricity excitation confined to the radial range swept by its orbit. Dynamical friction from the cluster reduces the orbital size of a $2000\,M_\odot$ IMBH initially at $0.15$~pc by more than a factor of two within $10$~Myr. Prograde IMBHs tend to align with the stellar disc, whereas a $2000\,M_\odot$ retrograde IMBH can anti-align with the radially overlapping disc stars. We also quantify how the IMBH affects the supply of stars to the loss cone and the rates of stellar tidal disruptions and binary separations.
#### The effect of triples on the Chemically Homogeneous Evolution Channel for producing Gravitational Wave Sources
 - **Authors:** Silvia A. Popa, Selma E. de Mink, Stephen Justham, Jakob Stegmann, Alejandro Vigna-Gomez, Lieke A. C. van Son
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14353

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14353

 - **Abstract**
 Despite the growing number of gravitational-wave (GW) detections, the formation of merging binary black holes (BBHs) is still debated. One of the proposed formation channels is Chemically Homogeneous Evolution (CHE), which makes distinct predictions for the properties of the resulting black holes (BHs): high masses ($\gtrsim35\, M_\odot$) and large, orbitally-aligned spins. These predictions are based on studies that consider this channel only in isolated binaries. However, most massive stars reside in triples. We investigate how a tertiary affects the CHE channel. We use detailed binary models computed with MESA for the inner CHE systems coupled with analytical prescriptions for the hierarchical triple dynamics. Specifically, we study the von Zeipel-Lidov-Kozai mechanism that can accelerate the merger of the inner BBHs through high-eccentricity excitations. We show that a tertiary companion enables mergers at higher metallicities, where the classical binary CHE channel does not operate. At $Z_\odot/3$, triples produce BHs with lower masses (around $15\,M_\odot$) and lower spins (around 0.25) compared to the low-metallicity classical scenario. We further expect triple dynamics to lead to spin-orbit misalignment and potential residual eccentricities at merger. In triples at $Z_\odot/3$, half of the mergers occur within 1 Gyr. Moreover, 10% of the population leads to prompt mergers within 1 Myr (1% within 1 kyr) that are of potential interest for electromagnetic counterparts. We conclude that the triple CHE channel is promising as (i) it may contribute more substantially to the local merger rate than had been expected for the classical CHE channel due to the shorter delay times and effectiveness at higher metallicities and (ii) produces mergers with properties that more closely resemble the lower masses and lower spins inferred for most of the observed GW merger population.

 - **Abstract**
 GS 1843+00 (Ginga 1843+009) is a poorly studied Be/X-ray binary system that underwent a major X-ray outburst in 2026 February-May after nearly sixteen years of quiescence. We present an X-ray, optical, and radio study of GS 1843+00 during this outburst, focusing on the evolution of the neutron star emission geometry, Be-disk connection, and possible outflow activity. We analyse three NuSTAR observations obtained at different stages of the outburst, complemented by archival RXTE and Chandra data, optical spectroscopy and imaging with the Nordic Optical Telescope, and a MeerKAT radio observation. Phase-resolved spectroscopy reveals variations in the Comptonization and blackbody parameters, indicating changes in the accretion-column structure. Optical spectroscopy confirms the presence of a Be-circumstellar disk through H$\alpha$ and Paschen-series emission lines. No radio counterpart is detected, with a $3\sigma$ upper limit of 36 $\mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$ at 1.28 GHz. Weaker H$\alpha$ emission compared with previous epochs indicates that the Be-circumstellar disk was in a different state during the current episode, while the radio non-detection suggests that any associated outflow activity was faint or below the detection limit.

 - **Abstract**
 Dwarf galaxies exhibit a large diversity in rotation curve shapes, challenging galaxy formation models within the $\Lambda$CDM paradigm. We present NIVARIA-LG, a new suite of cosmological simulations studying dwarf galaxy evolution in realistic Local Group environments. This intermediate-resolution pilot lays the groundwork for the full-scale NIVARIA project. The simulations combine constrained Local Group initial conditions from HESTIA with the NIHAO galaxy formation model.  - **Abstract**
 The rotating vector model (RVM) is widely used to interpret pulsar radio polarization under the assumption of a dipolar magnetic field. Motivated by tentative evidence for non-dipolar magnetic structure in millisecond pulsars provided by recent modeling of surface thermal X-ray pulsations, we examine how a global quadrupole component modifies position-angle (PA) behavior within the classical RVM framework. We numerically integrate magnetic field lines in static vacuum magnetospheres composed of a centered dipole component that is inclined with respect to the spin axis, and a centered quadrupole component aligned with it. We then compute the open field line regions (OFLRs), emission skymaps, and PA curves for a range of dipole inclinations, relative quadrupole strengths, and light cylinder radii corresponding to two trial spin periods of 1\,ms and 5\,ms.
